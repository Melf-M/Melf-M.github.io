Drug / Narcotic Crime Trends

This project examines how drug and narcotic-related crime in San Francisco changed from 2003 to 2025. The visualizations below move from the long-run trend to yearly variation and then to within-day timing patterns, creating a more narrative view of how these incidents evolved over time.

Question
How has drug / narcotic crime changed over time in San Fransico?

Part 1

The long-run trajectory

The first chart establishes the broadest pattern in the dataset. It gives a high-level view of how drug and narcotic incidents rose, fell, or stabilized across the full period before moving into more detailed distributional views.

Looking first at annual totals provides the clearest entry point into the story. Before asking how incidents vary by hour or by distribution, it helps to see the overall shape of change across two decades and identify the major turning points in the series.

Part 2

Variation across years

After the long-run trend, the next step is to ask how activity is distributed within each year. This adds nuance by showing spread, concentration, and irregularity that annual totals alone cannot capture.

A distributional chart helps reveal whether some years are tightly concentrated around certain time patterns while others are more diffuse or volatile. It shifts the emphasis from total volume to the shape of activity within each year.

Interpretation

Use this section to describe whether incidents are clustered or broadly spread across the day in each year, and whether that shape changes meaningfully over time. This is also a good place to comment on outliers, concentration, and volatility, then connect those observations back to the broader rise-and-fall pattern shown in the first chart.

  • Comment on whether spread becomes narrower or wider across different years.
  • Note skew, concentration, or outlier behavior where relevant.
  • Explain how these yearly shapes support or complicate the long-run trend story.
Part 3

Within-day timing patterns

This section narrows the focus from long-term yearly structure to the rhythm of incidents within the day, highlighting when activity tends to happen and whether those patterns remain consistent over time.

Timing patterns often reveal structure that is easy to miss in yearly aggregates. By shifting attention to the daily cycle, the visualization can show not only how much activity occurred, but when it tends to be concentrated and whether those concentrations change over time.

Interpretation

  • Describe the dominant hours or daily windows.
  • Point out any visible shifts or recurring regularities.
  • Show how timing patterns complement the story told by yearly totals.
