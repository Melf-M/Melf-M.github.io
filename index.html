San Francisco 2017–2025

Burglary in San Francisco (SF) before, during and after Covid-19

This project investigates changes in burglary incidents from 2017-2025 in San Francisco with a focus on the changes during Covid-19. To make comparison possible, the period is split into three periods; before covid; 2017-2019, covid; 2020-2022, after covid; 2023-2025. The analysis is based on two merged crime datasets available from San Francisco police department incident reports SF governments public and safety crime datasets. The analysis is based on districts inside of SF hence the district "Out of SF" is excluded.

Question
How did burglary in San Francisco change during a worldwide pandemic like Covid-19?
Time period
2017–2025
Visualizations
Static Trend lines, Animated choropleth map, Interactive violin/box plot

The long-run trajectory

The project start by establishing the general trend in burglary.

Interpretation

Northern, Richmond and Park show relatively large burglary incidents increase at the start of covid and peak in 2020 before decreasing steadily after the covid period. Central, Southern and Tenderloin show similar tendencies but on a smaller scale. Taraval and Ingleside see an increase during covid and peak in 2021, whereafter it remains stable until 2025 where it decreases to count levels that are lower than before covid. Bayview also has a small steady increase from 2020-2022 where it peaks in 2022 and decreases after.

Change in burglary incidents over the years

The project started by investigating the long term trends throgh raw incident counts. This next illustration aims to give an overview of how burglary incidents changed over time relative to a pre Covid baseline.

Interpretation

The map shows that most districts had similar burglary levels during 2017-2019, as expected since these years define the baseline. The first quarter of 2020 is like the baseline trend whereas burglaries rose massively during the second quarter. The largest increases happened in Park, Richmond and Northern while Mission, Southern, Central and Tenderloin also rose substantially. Most of these districts saw increases of 50-90% compared to baseline with Northern reaching 127% in one quarter. During the rest of 2020 and most of 2021 the same districts continued to show elevated burglary levels with almost all districts having a larger relative burglary level. Taraval, Ingleside and Bayview stay closer to their baseline at first, however Ingleside and Taraval also rose in parts of 2020 and 2021. By 2022 most districts began to move back toward their baseline, most within 30% of their baseline. There were still some districts like Richmond, Park and Mission having elevated levels in 2022, however in the period after Covid 2023-2025 all districts began to show the same trend as the baseline before Covid with 2025 having historical low incident levels compared to the baseline. Overall the map suggest that an increase in burglary levels were present throughout Covid, however only concentrated in a subset of districts.

Distribution of burglary incidents across years and districts

This section will take a deeper look into how the distribution of burglary incidents has changed throughout the years, without comparing directly to a baseline.

Interpretation

By exploring the interactive illustrations, all the districts see their peak year in terms of incidents during one of the covid period years. The most dominant being 2020 with 6 out of 10 districts have had the most incidents during this specific year. The boxplots reveal that the median is usually around 2021 meaning that for the majority of districts half of the burglary incidents occur before and after 2021. By further investigation we can also see that most of the boxes have IQR from 2019 to 2023 with a few deviations of Richmond (2020-2023), Central (2019-2022) and (Bayview 2018-2023). Sorting the districts by highest covid share it is seen that many districts that were revealed to have an increase in the covid period from the choropleth map are indeed also the ones with the largest covid incident share namely, Park, Northern, Richmond, Mission. These districts together with Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval have similar incident shares before and after covid with an increase during Covid. In contrast Central, Southern and Bayview show a distribution that is flatter with similar shares before and during covid with a decrease in incidents after Covid. The violins shapes support this observation, especially for Park, Northern, Richmond and Mission show large concentration around the Covid period, while Bayview appear more uniform and Central, Southern show a milder peak followed by a decline after 2021. Lastly Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval also show larger concentration during Covid, however the distribution appears a little more uniform. Overall, the figure supports that there was an increase in incidents during Covid-19 for many districts, however the magnitude was very different.

Reflection & Conclusion

What changed during Covid-19?

In conclusion, overall patterns suggest that burglary in San Francisco saw changes during the covid period, though the strength of that change were different across police districts.

Overall, the findings suggest that covid did not affect all districts of San Francisco, but it seems to be associated with an increase of burglary in several districts such as Richmond, Park and Northern. Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval also had an increase during Covid which might suggest that these districts also saw change but not as much. Finally, it is hard to conclude that districts such as Central, Southern and Bayview were impacted during covid since their incident share was very similar to the years preceding covid. These findings are consistent with multiple articles on the subject. SFGATE's article One SF neighborhood sees 75% increase in burglaries explain how all districts saw an increase in burglary incidents with Park experiencing the largest bump. The article points out that the SFPD stated that they do not have an "exact cause" for the increase, but it is clear why more people are in their homes when the thieves break in. Further sources like Explore: How crime changed in 2022 explains how there was an increase in residential burglaries that was unique to San Francisco compared to other large cities stated by police director Magnus Lofstrom. The article mentions that burglaries rose by over 50% during the period between 2019 and 2020 where Lofstrom explains the unusual increase in the city by many San Franciscans deciding to live and work outside the city in the early pandemic, leaving properties unguarded. Lastly Crime in San Francisco: What You Need to Know shows SF burglary rates from 2018 to 2025 with both residential and non-residential burglary seeing a large increase from 2018/2019 to 2020 and peaking in 2021 whereafter it begins to decrease steadily toward 2025.
