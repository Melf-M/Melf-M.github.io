Covid-19 impact on burglary in SF

The focus of this project is to examine, analyze and visualize how the crime of burglary has changed from 2017-2025, specifically how covid-19 impacted the trend. The time range is split into three periods each consisting of three years such that the basis for comparison is fair. The periods are defined as; before covid; 2017-2019, covid; 2020-2022, after covid; 2023-2025. The data comes from the SF governments public and safety crime datasets where "Police Department Incident Reports: 2018 to Present" and "Police Department Incident Reports: Historical 2003 to May 2018" has been merged. The merged dataset consists of the different SF police departments reported incidents from 2003-2026. To enable the analysis in this project, burglary is picked out as a focus crime and the time is restricted to 2017-2025 such that we can evaluate the impact of covid. The project considers all police districts inside of the San Fransico area hence the district "Out of SF" is removed.

Question
How has a world wide pandemic like covid-19 impacted burglary in San Fransico?
Time period
2017–2025

Variation of burglary incidents across years throughout districts

We started by investigating the long term trends throgh raw incident counts. This next illustration aims to give a more naunced overview of how covid affected the amount of burglary incidents throughout the years.

This choropleth map shows the spatio temporal distribution of burglary across the SF plice districts in our time period. Each district is colored according to its burglary level in a given quarter of each year relative to its own average 2017-2019 baseline. The metric is calculated by $relative level = \frac{current quarter count}{baseline average count}$. This means that a value above 1 means that the district experienced more burglary than the baseline while a value less than 1 indicate less burglary than the baseline e.g. a value of 1.2 means the burglary count is 20% higher than the district's 2017-2019 average. The animated map makes it possible to see how the burglary patterns change over time for each district. Note that since the measure is normalized based on each districts own historical baseline, the visualization illustrates the relative change from the past to the present, meaning it tells how strongly each districts were affected over time.

Distributional focus of burglary incidents over the years

This section will take a deeper look into how the distribution of burglary incidents has changed throughout the years. The focus in this section is about exploring the distributional changes and not only comparing covid years directly to a historical baseline but also to the years after covid to get a broader overview.

The figure is an interactive visualization which show both the boxplot of incidents throughout the years but also the distributon of the incident counts through a violin plot. It is possibly to hover over the different years in each district to get different statistics on a year to year basis which reveal important insight into the data. The figure allows toggling different districts and zooming such that one can isolate interesting districts and further one can sort the districts by different metrics to investigate different focus points.

Interpretation

By exploring the interactive illustrations it is clear that all the districts see their peak year in terms of incident count during one of the covid period years. The most dominant being 2020 with 6/10 districts having their most incidents during this specific year. The boxplots reveal that the median is usually around 2021 meaning that for the majority of districts half of the burglary incidents occur before and after 2021. By further investigation we can see that most of the boxes has their IQR from 2019 to 2023 with a few deviations of Richmond having 2020-2023, Central 2019-2022 and Bayview 2018-2023. This further supports that 50% of the burglary incidents are concentrated around the covid period, only with Bayview having a larger IQR meaning that the incident counts are more spread through the years preceeding covid. This incidates that there could be some overrepresentation during the covid period however it is not yet enough imformation since we have to investigate how much of the incident shares lie in the years 2019 and 20223. By sorting the districts by highest covid share it is seen that many districts that were revealed to have an increase in the covid period from the choropleth map are indeed also the ones with the largest covid incident share; Park, Northern, Richmond, Mission. However looking at the % share of incidents before, during and after covid it is observed that almost all districts has rather interesting distributional changes of the incidents. Districts like Park, Northern, Richond, Mission, Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval has very similar % incident share both before and after covid, with a significant increase of the share during the covid period, whereas Central, Southern and Bayview has very large % share of incidents before and during covid with a very large decrease in incidents after covid. Looking at the violins this is further supported as the distributions of incidents for Central and Southern which show that the incident distributions are similar during 2017-2022 with a peak during 2020, whereafter the incident counts begin to decrease from 2022-2025. Bayview has a more uniform distribution throughout 2017-2022 with a peak during 2022 and afterward the incidents begin to decrease. Violins for districts like Park, Northern, Richmond and Mission show a clear pattern with lower incident counts during before and after covid and a significant spike in the distribution during the covid period. These districts all have over 40% of their total incidents occuring during the covid period hence general majority share compared to both before and after. Lastly Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval show similar distributional patterns with more a larger incident share during covid and a more stabilized pattern before and after covid, however these districts are a little more uniform.

  • Park, Northern, Richmond, Mission appear to have a significant increase in the share of burglary incidents during covid compared to both before and after.
  • Tenderloin, Ingleside, Taraval appear to have a slight increase in the shary of burglary incidents during covid compared to both before and after.
  • Central, Southern, Taravel does not appear to have an increase in the share of burglary incidents during covid compared to before. There is however a clear decrease in incidents after covid.
Conclusion

What changed during Covid-19?

To conclude the findings in this page with the trend plot, the relative quarterly map, and the yearly distribution view, the overall pattern suggests that burglary in San Francisco was partly affected during the covid period, though the strength of that effect differed across police districts.

The yearly trend lines gave an overview of the districts and showed that many districts experienced increases of burglary upon entering covid. Several districts showing peaks of around 2020 or 2021 before beginning to decline afterward again. The quarterly choropleth plot supported this by giving an overview of how the districts rose above their own 2017-2019 baseline, specially during 2020 and 2021. Lastly in the distributional analysis it was shown that incidents were often concentrated around the covid years in the boxplots. Upon further analysis it was revealed in the violins that some district distributional shapes peaked during covid and had significant increases compared to both before and after. Districts such as Park, Northern, Richmond and Mission appear to have been affected the most with clear incident increase in 2020-2022, while Central, Southern and Bayview were more stable and harder to distinguish whether covid had any impact.

Overall the findings suggest that covid did not affect all districts of San Fransico but it seems to be associated with a significant and systematic increase of burglary in several districts such as Richmond, Park, Northern and Richmond. Tenderloin, Ingleside and Taraval also had an increase during covid which might suggest that these districts were also affected but not in the same level as the others. Finally it is hard to conclude that districts such as Central, Southern and Bayview were impacted during covid since their incident share were very similar to the years preceeding covid.
